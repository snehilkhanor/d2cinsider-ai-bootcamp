# Customer Voice Report, Gur & Ghee

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Source:** `brand-brain/reviews.md`, 32 messages, 2 May to 23 May 2026. 20 site and Google reviews, 6 Zomato and Swiggy reviews, 6 WhatsApp messages.
**Sample note:** 32 is just over the line. Treat counts of 3 as signals, counts of 6 as patterns.

## Top read in 5 lines

1. The reason people love the product is one phrase, repeated: **"not too sweet"**. It appears in 6 of 32 messages and it is not on the homepage.
2. The buyer is very often not the eater. 6 of 32 are about sending it to parents or serving it to guests, and the proof of success is **the parent's approval**.
3. **Three customers asked if the laddoos are safe for a diabetic parent**, and one quoted the pack: "It says no sugar." Our wording is creating that belief. This is the most urgent finding in the report.
4. Summer shipping is costing gifts, not just boxes: 3 melted or soft arrivals, average 1.7 stars, and one says it is "not giftable like this".
5. Khajur Gur Bites has the best rating in the range (4.75) and the worst conversion (1.4%). Parents who try it love it. The page is not getting them to try it.

## Themes

### 1. Not too sweet, tastes like home (6 of 32)

Reviews 1, 3, 7, 11, 19, 21.

> "Not too sweet. Tastes like what my nani made in winters."
> "Finally something that isn't sugar syrup. You can taste the til."
> "Reminds me of Sankranti at my dadi's house. Not sticky sweet."

Customer words to reuse: *not too sweet, not sticky sweet, you can taste the til, nani, dadi, Sankranti*. Nobody says jaggery. They say gur.

### 2. Sent to parents, served to guests, and it was approved (6 of 32)

Reviews 2, 8, 15, 18, 22, 24.

> "Papa called to say it's the first online mithai he didn't complain about."
> "They asked where it is from, which never happens."
> "The handwritten card made my mother cry."

Two of the six ordered from abroad for delivery in Rajasthan. The emotional payoff is the phone call afterwards.

### 3. Children treat it as a sweet, parents treat it as a relief (4 of 32)

Reviews 6, 13, 20, 26. All Khajur Gur Bites.

> "My 6 year old asks for these instead of chocolate after dinner."
> "Tiffin treat sorted. My daughter thinks it's a toffee."

No parent used the word healthy. They said *instead of chocolate* and *tiffin*.

### 4. Small for the price (4 of 32)

Reviews 3, 13, 14, 25.

> "12 small laddoos for 649 is steep."
> "Tasty but small portion for the price."

Never the reason for a low rating. Every one of these is 4 stars. It is a grumble attached to praise.

### 5. Arrived soft, oily or melted (3 of 32)

Reviews 5, 10, 17. Hyderabad, Nagpur, Chennai. Ratings 2, 1 and 2.

> "Melted mess. Ghee had leaked through the box. Why ship this in May?"
> "Taste was fine after I put them in the fridge but it's not giftable like this."

All three are ghee laddoos. No chikki, makhana or khajur order mentions heat.

### 6. Is it safe for a diabetic? (3 of 32)

Messages 27, 29, 31. All WhatsApp, all before purchase, all asking on behalf of a parent.

> "Is the dry fruit laddoo okay for my father, he is diabetic? It says no sugar."
> "Hi, are these sugar free? My mother has sugar."

**Flag for the claim wall.** "No refined sugar" is being read as "no sugar". See Discrepancies.

### 7. Besan laddoo is dry (3 of 32)

Reviews 4, 12, 23. All 3 stars.

> "Dry. Needed chai to get through it."

All three like the flavour. This is either a recipe question or an expectation the page sets wrongly.

### 8. Slow delivery and silent tracking (3 of 32)

Reviews 9, 16 and message 30. Chennai, Indore, and one unnamed.

> "Delivery took 7 days and no tracking update for 4 of them."

One of the three was a gift with a date attached.

### Below the line: corporate buyers cannot find the door (2 of 32)

Messages 28 and 32. Two is not a theme. But both are orders of 60 boxes or more, one is monthly, and both asked the same question: who do I speak to?

## Cohort cuts

- **Heat complaints are all outside Rajasthan and all ghee laddoos.** Local delivery has none.
- **Every diabetic question arrives on WhatsApp before purchase.** None are in reviews, which means the site is not answering it.
- **Gifting reviews are the longest and most specific.** They are also the ones with a named second person in them.

## Personas

**The sender.** 32 to 48, lives away from home, parents in a tier 2 city. Buys the Til Gur Laddoo or the Festive Box. Success is a phone call from a parent. Fears a box that arrives late or looks bad. *"Papa called to say it's the first online mithai he didn't complain about."*

**The tiffin parent.** Child aged 4 to 10. Buys Khajur Gur Bites. Wants something the child accepts as a treat. Does not want a lecture about nutrition. *"My daughter thinks it's a toffee."*

**The caring child of a diabetic parent.** Wants to send a sweet to a parent with diabetes and is hoping we are the answer. We are not, and we have to say so kindly. *"My mother has sugar."*

## Discrepancies with CLAUDE.md

1. `CLAUDE.md` allows "no refined sugar" when it sits next to "sweetened with jaggery". Three customers still read it as safe for diabetics. The rule is being followed and it is not working.
2. `CLAUDE.md` lists the tiffin parent as secondary. On rating and enthusiasm they are the strongest group in this data.
3. `CLAUDE.md` does not mention delivery speed anywhere. Three customers did.

## Founder asks

Only you can answer these.

1. Is the besan laddoo meant to be dry, the way some families make it, or is this a batch problem?
2. What do you currently reply when someone asks about diabetes? That reply should be on the product page, not only in WhatsApp.
3. Would you sell a 24 piece pack of Khajur Gur Bites? Two of four reviewers asked for more.
4. Who answers a corporate enquiry today, and how fast?
