## Section 3. Story

- **Why this brand exists**: My nani made til gur laddoos every Sankranti and never wrote the recipe down because there was nothing to write. When she died, the shops near us were selling laddoos made with refined sugar and vanaspati and calling them the same thing. I spent eleven years in bank operations learning how to make a process repeatable. In 2023 I used that on her recipe. We make to order, I taste every batch, and we sell nine things.
- **What we will never do**:
  - We never discount. Not at Diwali, not for a win-back, not for five boxes. We offer free shipping or a handwritten card instead.
  - We never call ourselves healthy, sugar free or good for anyone's condition. Gur is sugar.
  - We never ship a box we would not gift ourselves. If the weather will ruin it, we do not send it.

### Brand voice rules

**Always**:

- Name the ingredient, do not adjective it. "Roasted white sesame, Kolhapuri gur, a spoon of ghee."
- Say the hard thing first. "They are sweet. Gur is sugar, we are not going to pretend otherwise."
- Short sentences. One dry line per piece, at most. "A job I gave myself and will not be delegating."
- Gur, til, nani, dadi, tiffin, in plain type. No italics, no translation.
- When we got it wrong, the first sentence says so. "That is on us, not the courier."

**Never**:

- "Healthy", "guilt-free", "sugar free", "diabetic friendly", "immunity", "superfood"
- "Authentic", "artisanal", "handcrafted with love", "premium", "indulge"
- Any discount language: "% off", "sale", "offer ends", "use code"
- "Elevate", "unlock", "symphony of flavours", "in today's fast-paced world"
- Exclamation marks. Emoji in anything except a WhatsApp reply, and then one.

**Reading age**: Class 8. Indian English.

### Compliance and safety

The claim wall. Every customer-facing line is checked against this table.

| Claim | Status | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| "No refined sugar" | Allowed, with care | Must sit next to "sweetened with jaggery" in the same line or the line directly below |
| "No added sugar", "sugar free" | Never | Jaggery is an added sugar |
| "Diabetic friendly", "safe for diabetics", "low GI" | Never | If a customer asks, say plainly that gur is sugar and they should ask their doctor |
| "Healthy", "nutritious", "good for immunity", "good for digestion" | Never | Health claims need substantiation we do not have |
| "Gluten free" | Never, on any product | Besan has no gluten but our kitchen handles wheat. No product is tested |
| "No preservatives" | Allowed | True for all nine products. Recheck if a recipe changes |
| "Made with ghee" | Allowed | We use cow ghee from one named dairy. Do not write "A2" or "bilona", we cannot prove either |
| "Handmade", "small batch" | Allowed | True. Batches are 40 boxes |
| Kids' claims: "good for children", "healthy tiffin" | Never | Say what it is and that children like it. Nothing about benefit |

- **Allergens, on every pack and product page**: sesame, peanuts, tree nuts, milk. "Made in a kitchen that also handles wheat."
- **Shelf life**: stated in days from the date on the box, never "fresh for weeks".
- **FSSAI**: licence number on every pack and in the site footer. Tracked by the Ops Lead.

**Named risk.** Three of thirty-two customer messages in May read "no refined sugar" as "safe for a diabetic parent". That is our wording causing it. Until the pack and page copy is fixed, this is the one line most likely to hurt a customer and the brand.
